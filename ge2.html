📋 Course Overview

This is an Intermediate level course (CEFR B1) that develops academic skills in Reading, Writing, Listening and Speaking, and equips students with the grammar and vocabulary needed to progress to General English 3 (GE3).

Course codeFPGE0002
PrerequisiteGE1, or equivalent Placement Test score
Contact hours18 hrs/week
10 hrs Reading & Writing + 8 hrs Listening & Speaking (both with integrated Grammar & Lexis)
TextbooksPathways 2: Reading, Writing & Critical Thinking · Pathways 2: Listening, Speaking & Critical Thinking · UTAS Supplementary Materials (STMs)

Course Objectives

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

  1. Recognize the main ideas and details of complex reading and listening texts related to social, academic and work-related topics.
  2. Interact with reading and listening texts using higher-order thinking skills (inferencing, evaluating, synthesizing).
  3. Interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with speakers possible without strain.
  4. Produce clear, detailed written texts on a range of subjects and explain a viewpoint on real-world, academic and work-related topics.
  5. Demonstrate a level-appropriate accuracy and range of grammatical structures.
  6. Access and use virtual platforms for independent language learning.

🎯 Learning Outcomes

What students should be able to do in each skill by the end of GE2.

🎧 Listening

  1. Use pre-listening strategies to preview, activate prior knowledge, predict content and identify keywords/cues in complex texts.
  2. Listen to a dialogue or monologue of 4–5 minutes delivered in clear, standard speech.
  3. Extract the main idea and specific information from recorded texts on familiar academic, local, national or international matters.
  4. Understand speech expressing agreement, disagreement and contrasting opinions.
  5. Take notes while listening to a lecture or talk on college/university-level topics.
  6. Understand high-frequency idioms and grasp colloquial language in a clear context.
  7. Identify the meaning of unknown words from context.
  8. Use inference skills to identify speakers' viewpoints, feelings and attitudes.
  9. Evaluate the content of listening texts related to academic and real-world topics.
  10. Synthesize the content of listening texts orally or in writing.

📖 Reading

  1. Use pre-reading strategies to preview, activate prior knowledge and establish a purpose for reading.
  2. Comprehend level-appropriate texts of around 500 words, recognizing main ideas and specific details.
  3. Recognize the reading purpose and adopt an appropriate style (skimming, scanning or intensive reading).
  4. Use context clues to guess the meaning of unfamiliar words.
  5. Demonstrate a range of level-appropriate actively understood vocabulary.
  6. Identify main ideas, supporting details and relevant information in everyday materials (emails, newspapers, magazines, infographics, online documents).
  7. Understand current and historical information regarding processes.
  8. Understand texts involving agreement/disagreement, comparison/contrast, and causes/effects.
  9. Evaluate the content of reading texts related to academic and real-world topics.
  10. Synthesize the content of reading texts orally or in writing.

🗣️ Speaking

  1. Use pre-speaking strategies to communicate without hesitation, activating prior knowledge and unlocking difficult words.
  2. Use increasing fluency and accuracy, combining simple and complex sentences in social, academic and work-related settings.
  3. Participate in impromptu and small-group speaking activities.
  4. Demonstrate willingness to paraphrase in order to express ideas.
  5. Provide reasons and explanations for opinions and plans, illustrating critical thinking.
  6. Prepare and deliver a talk of 3–5 minutes on social, academic or work-related topics.
  7. Use pronunciation, intonation and stress well enough to avoid placing strain on listeners.
  8. Demonstrate increased mastery of phonemes that are problematic for Arabic speakers.
  9. Request repetition or examples when the meaning of something is unclear.

✍️ Writing

  1. Use prewriting strategies to generate and develop ideas and make a plan before writing.
  2. Write an essay of at least 200 words with an introduction, two or more supporting paragraphs, and a conclusion.
  3. Write simple, compound and complex sentences using discourse markers (time sequence, cause & effect, comparison & contrast).
  4. Describe a process with appropriate discourse markers, using at least 150 words.
  5. Write a report of an incident with appropriate discourse markers, using at least 150 words.
  6. Write an essay discussing causes and effects, and give an opinion.
  7. Write an essay comparing and contrasting, then give an opinion.

🔤 Vocabulary

  1. Pronounce and spell level-appropriate CEFR B1 vocabulary correctly.
  2. Pronounce and spell correctly the names of countries, cities, nationalities, occupations, animals, colours, dates, academic subjects and simple activities.
  3. Demonstrate an increased mastery of derived word forms such as prefixes and suffixes.
  4. Understand synonyms and antonyms.

Midterm & Final Exam

Both exams cover Reading, Listening and Writing, administered individually and collected in order (Reading → Listening → Writing) in one session with no break. The Final Exam adds a Speaking component (interview, two examiners), with all four skills weighted equally at 25 marks each. Bring your student card and arrive at least 30 minutes early.

Writing Portfolio (10% · 40 marks)

Two in-class writing tasks — one before the MTE, one after. For each task you write a first draft, get teacher feedback (ungraded), then revise it into a second draft during class time. Only the second draft is graded. Task 1: 25 minutes · Task 2: 40 minutes.

Debate (5% · 25 marks)

A pair-vs-pair debate, 6 minutes per session (8 minutes for a group of three), held in Weeks 11–12. You're assessed individually on five criteria: Information, Rebuttal, Use of Facts/Statistics, Organization, and Understanding of Topic (5 points each).

E-Learning Activities (5% · 14 marks)

14 activities on SPARK/Moodle (7 for Reading & Writing, 7 for Listening & Speaking), spread across the semester before and after the MTE. Each completed activity earns 1 mark — automatic grading gives you immediate feedback.

📅 Attendance

Attendance is recorded at the start of every lecture — arriving late counts as absent, except for the 8:00 a.m. class, which has a 10-minute grace period. You're responsible for monitoring your own percentage through the online system.

Valid excuses (must be supported by official documents)

  • Illness: a sick-leave certificate from a government hospital/polyclinic (private certificates must be certified by a government health centre).
  • Appointments: inform your teacher in advance and provide valid proof.
  • Death of a first-degree relative: an official document, submitted within 3 days.
  • College activities: with prior approval from the DAVC-ESSS.
Mass absence — not permitted; it disrupts the delivery plan. Everyone present is still marked, and learning outcomes covered that day are not repeated for those who missed it.

📐 Exam & Other Rules

  • Bring a valid ID — university, civil, driving licence, or a digital ID such as the Royal Oman Police app.
  • Arrive within 30 minutes of the start; late arrivals get no extra time. After 30 minutes, entry is refused unless a valid excuse allows a make-up.
  • Papers can only be submitted after half the exam time has passed, and only once — answers cannot be changed afterwards.
  • Missed a Continuous Assessment component? Submit a valid excuse to the PSC Unit Head within 3 days to be granted a make-up.
  • Missing the make-up exam results in an F grade for that component.
  • Cheating leads to a warning; repeated behaviour results in withdrawal from the exam and further disciplinary action.

🛡️ Academic Integrity

Cheating, attempting to cheat, or plagiarizing (using another author's words without crediting them) has no value and is never tolerated at UTAS. These rules apply to quizzes, exams, reports, coursework, and research/graduation projects.

  • Caught cheating or attempting to cheat → stopped from continuing that exam (online or in person).
  • Confirmed cheating → a Fail grade for the course.
  • You will not be allowed to register the following semester.
  • A third offence results in dismissal from the university.
  • Plagiarism can lead to failing the assignment/test, failing the course, probation, suspension, or expulsion.

Discipline & Order

All GFP students must follow the university dress code and its rules of academic conduct at all times — in lectures, exam halls, and elsewhere. Disciplinary penalties for dishonesty, threats, or assault can lead to official withdrawal from the university. Health, safety, and respect for university property and other people's rights must always be observed.

⚖️ Appeals

  • Submit a request to review your final grade (in any component) to the Admission and Registration Centre.
  • You have 3 working days from the date the final result is announced.
  • One free appeal per course — fees apply to appeals on additional courses.
  • Appeals are decided by the end of the first week of the following semester.
  • No response by then means the appeal is considered rejected.
